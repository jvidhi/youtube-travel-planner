This highly detailed, location-aware 14-day itinerary is custom-tailored for a group of friends in their 20s traveling on a budget. It perfectly balances **high-octane adventure, anime culture, legendary nightlife, and deep-nature relaxation**, while aligning seamlessly with two major events: the **September Sumo Tournament** and the wild **Kishiwada Danjiri Matsuri**.

To make this trip highly realistic and budget-friendly, this itinerary utilizes smart transit hacks:
*   An **overnight highway bus** from Tokyo to Osaka (saving you both a night of accommodation and the high cost of a Shinkansen ticket).
*   A **group car rental** for the rugged terrain of Shikoku’s Iya Valley (highly cost-effective when split among friends).
*   The **JAL Free Domestic Flight Hack** to fly back from Shikoku to Tokyo for $0.

# 🏙️ Phase 1: Tokyo – Anime, Nightlife, & Sumo (Days 1–4)

### Day 1: Welcome to Tokyo – Historic Vibes & Social Lounge
*   **Morning/Afternoon:** Arrive at Tokyo Haneda (HND) or Narita (NRT) Airport. Take the direct Keisei Access Express train straight to Kuramae Station. Check into **Nui. Hostel & Bar Lounge** (Asakusa), a stunningly designed budget hostel featuring a massive industrial-chic downstairs bar lounge that draws a lively mix of backpackers and local Tokyoites.
*   **Late Afternoon:** Walk 15 minutes to **Senso-ji Temple** in Asakusa. Visiting at dusk is a great budget hack: the massive crowds are gone, the temple is beautifully illuminated (completely free), and the atmospheric side streets are perfect for photos.
*   **Evening:** Head back to Nui. Hostel's lounge. Grab a cheap local craft beer, meet fellow travelers, and plan your upcoming days. 

### Day 2: The Otaku Pilgrimage & Shibuya Crossing
*   **Morning:** Take the Toei Oedo Line from Kuramae to Okachimachi Station, then walk to **Akihabara**. Sundays are best as the main road is pedestrian-only, but any day is great for exploring. Dive into the 8-story **Mandarake Complex** for retro toys and search for rare capsule toys at **Gachapon Kaikan**.
*   **Afternoon:** Hop on the Sobu Line to Yotsuya Station. Walk 10 minutes to the **Suga Shrine Staircase** to recreate the iconic final scene from Makoto Shinkai's masterpiece *Your Name* (free!).
*   **Evening:** Take the metro to Shibuya. Snap a photo on the **Shibuya Crossing**, then head to the narrow alleyways of **Nonbei Yokocho** or grab cheap draft beers and yakitori skewers at a local *Tachinomi* (standing bar) like *Shibuya Nomibe Kerr*. 
    *   *Insider App Tip:* Use **Google Maps** to navigate Tokyo's massive train networks. It will tell you exactly which carriage to board for the fastest transfer.

### Day 3: Vintage Anime Treasures & Giant Robots
*   **Morning:** Take the JR Chuo Line to Nakano Station. Head straight into **Nakano Broadway**, a multi-story retro shopping complex. It is significantly cheaper than Akihabara for anime figurines, vintage manga, and retro video games.
*   **Afternoon:** Take the train to **Odaiba DiverCity**. Stand in awe of the life-sized **Unicorn Gundam Statue** (it transforms at 11:00 AM, 1:00 PM, 3:00 PM, and 5:00 PM). Explore the real-life locations in Odaiba featured in *Digimon Adventure*.
*   **Evening:** Head to Shinjuku. Grab cheap dinner sets (*Teishoku*) under ¥1,000 (~$7 USD) at *Y弥生軒 (Yayoi-ken)*, then explore **Omoide Yokocho** (Memory Lane) and the tiny, neon-lit bars of **Golden Gai**. 

### Day 4: Mt. Fuji Views & High-Energy Sumo
*   **Morning:** Take the Keio Line from Shinjuku Station directly to Takaosanguchi Station (50 mins, ~¥390). Hike Trail 1 or 4 up **Mount Takao**. On a clear September day, the summit offers breathtaking views of Mount Fuji. Take the cable car halfway down to save your knees.
*   **Afternoon (2:00 PM):** Head to **Ryogoku Kokugikan** for the **September Sumo Tournament** (Sept 13–27, 2026). General admission tickets can be bought online in advance for a very reasonable price. Watching these 300-pound athletes collide is a high-energy, unforgettable cultural experience.
*   **Evening:** Head to the trendy neighborhood of **Shimokitazawa** (just west of Shibuya/Shinjuku). It's the epicenter of Tokyo's indie music, vintage clothing, and budget-friendly curry shops.

# 🌲 Phase 2: Minakami – The Mountain Adventure Capital (Days 5–6)

### Day 5: Journey to the Mountains & Bridge Bungee
*   **Morning:** Check out of Nui. Hostel. To save money, take the JR Takasaki Line from Ueno to Takasaki, then transfer to the Joetsu Line to Minakami Station (approx. 2.5 hours, ~¥3,000). 
*   **Afternoon:** Check into **Tenjin Lodge**, a highly-rated mountain lodge with budget-friendly dorms, a communal bathhouse, and a rustic bar. Walk or take a local bus to the **Suwakyo Bridge**. Gear up for a heart-pounding 42-meter bungee jump over the rushing Tone River gorge.
*   **Evening:** Soak in a traditional public bath (*Sento*) in town for around ¥500 to soothe your muscles, followed by a hearty hot pot dinner at the lodge.

### Day 6: Extreme Canyoning & The Overnight Bus Hack
*   **Morning (8:30 AM):** Head to **Canyons Minakami**, the premier outdoor adventure center in Japan. Gear up for the **Fox Canyon Canyoning Course**, which features a massive 20-meter natural stone waterfall slide and cliff jumps into crystal-clear mountain pools.
*   **Afternoon:** Grab a cheap post-adventure burger and local craft beer at the **I Love Outdoors Cafe**.
*   **Evening (The Budget Hack):** Take the Joetsu Line back to Tokyo (Shinjuku). Grab dinner, and head to the Shinjuku Expressway Bus Terminal (Busta Shinjuku). Board an **overnight highway bus** (like *Willer Express*) bound for Osaka.
    *   *Why this works:* Tickets cost between ¥3,500 and ¥6,000 (compared to a ¥14,000 Shinkansen). You sleep on the bus, saving the cost of a hostel night and arriving in Osaka refreshed and ready to go!

# 🐙 Phase 3: Osaka & The Danjiri Matsuri (Days 7–11)

### Day 7: Welcome to the Street Food Capital
*   **Morning:** Arrive at Osaka Umeda or Namba Station. Walk to **Backstage Osaka Hostel & Bar** (near Nippombashi Station) to drop off your bags. 
*   **Afternoon:** Explore **America-mura (Amemura)**, the heart of Osaka's youth culture. It is packed with cheap thrift stores, massive street art murals, and young locals hanging out. Grab a cheap lunch of *long soft-serve ice cream* or takoyaki.
*   **Evening:** Walk to **Dotonbori**. Feast on cheap, world-class street food: try the legendary melt-in-your-mouth octopus balls at **Takoyaki Wanaka Dotonbori** (approx. ¥600 for 8 pieces) and crispy skewers at *Kushikatsu Daruma*. 

### Day 8: Retro Gaming & Neon Alleyways
*   **Morning:** Walk to **Den Den Town (Nippombashi)**, Osaka’s anime district. It is less crowded than Tokyo's Akihabara and often has much better deals on retro Game Boys, Nintendo games, and anime figurines.
*   **Afternoon:** Walk through the massive **Osaka Castle Park** (completely free to explore the stunning stone walls and outer moats). 
*   **Evening:** Head to **Shinsekai**, a retro neighborhood built in 1912 to mimic Paris and New York. It is beautifully gritty and neon-lit. Grab dinner at a local standing bar (*Tachinomi*) where you can get cheap draft beer and yakitori while chatting with locals.

### Day 9: Low-Budget Kyoto Day Trip
*   **Morning:** Take the JR Special Rapid Service from Osaka Station to Kyoto Station (only 30 minutes, ¥580). Rent cheap city bicycles near the station.
*   **Afternoon:** Ride to the **Kiyomizu-dera** district and explore the historic wooden streets of Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka. 
*   **Late Afternoon/Dusk:** Ride to **Fushimi Inari Shrine**. This is a major budget and crowd hack: hike the thousands of vibrant red Torii gates at sunset. The crowds disappear, the mountain paths are lit by stone lanterns, and it is completely free and open 24/7.
*   **Evening:** Return to Osaka for a late-night ramen run at *Ichiran* or a local 24-hour diner.

### Day 10: Rest & Recharge at Spa World
*   **Daytime:** After several days of heavy walking, take a rest day. Head to **SPAWORLD Hotel & Resort** in Shinsekai. For around ¥1,500–¥2,000, you get access to a massive, multi-story thermal theme park featuring European and Asian-themed hot springs, saunas, and relaxation areas. It is the ultimate budget relaxation experience.
*   **Evening:** Walk around the local food stalls in Shinsekai and pack your bags for the festival tomorrow.

### Day 11: Kishiwada Danjiri Matsuri (The Wildest Festival)
*   **Morning (8:00 AM):** Take the Nankai Main Line from Namba Station to **Kishiwada Station** (25 mins, ~¥500). 
*   **All Day:** Experience the **Kishiwada Danjiri Matsuri** (September 19–20, 2026). This is Japan’s most high-adrenaline festival. Teams of local men sprint through tight, narrow streets pulling massive, 4-ton wooden carts (*danjiri*). Watch in awe as guild leaders perform acrobatic, gravity-defying dances on the moving roofs as they whip around corners at full speed.
    *   *Crucial YouTube Insight:* As highlighted by traveler *Arisa Mameda*, you will easily clock over **30,000 steps** trying to follow the action, so wear your most comfortable athletic shoes! Eat cheap yakisoba, karaage, and choko-bananas from the hundreds of festival street stalls.
*   **Evening:** Head back to Backstage Osaka Hostel for a post-festival party at their lively social bar.

# 🌿 Phase 4: Iya Valley – The Secluded Wilderness (Days 12–14)

### Day 12: Journey into the "Lost World" of Shikoku
*   **Morning (7:30 AM):** Take the direct Highway Bus operated by Hankyu/Shikoku Kotsu from Hankyu Sanbangai Terminal (Umeda) to **Awaikeda Bus Terminal** in Tokushima Prefecture (approx. 4 hours, ~¥4,800).
*   **Afternoon:** Rent a budget K-car (light car) near Awa-Ikeda Station. Splitting a K-car among a group of friends is incredibly cheap and essential for navigating the steep, winding mountain roads of the Iya Valley. 
*   **Late Afternoon:** Drive deep into the valley to the **Iya Valley Vine Bridge (Kazurabashi)**. Pay a small fee (~¥550) to cross this historic, heart-stopping bridge made of mountain vines woven together, hanging 14 meters over a rushing wild river.
*   **Evening:** Check into a local budget guesthouse or traditional farmhouse. Cook a communal dinner over an open hearth (*irori*) and enjoy the absolute peace of the mountains.
    *   *Insider App Tip:* Use the **Google Translate live camera feature** to translate hand-written menus and signs in this remote region.

### Day 13: Wild White-Water Rafting & Scarecrow Village
*   **Morning (9:00 AM):** Head to **Happyraft** (located in nearby Otoyo, Kochi). Embark on a world-class white-water rafting trip down the Yoshino River's Oboke and Koboke Gorges. The rapids here are legendary, offering some of the best rafting in Asia.
*   **Afternoon:** Drive to the bizarre and fascinating **Nagoro Scarecrow Village**. Walk through a valley where life-sized, hand-made scarecrows outnumber the human residents 10-to-1, placed in lifelike poses in schools, bus stops, and fields. Stop by the **Peeing Boy Statue**, perched precariously on a 200-meter-high cliff edge overlooking the emerald waters of the Iya Gorge.
*   **Evening:** Unwind with a riverside BBQ and a bottle of local Shikoku sake.

### Day 14: Fly Free to Tokyo & Departure
*   **Morning:** Drive from Iya Valley to **Takamatsu Airport (TAK)** or **Tokushima Airport (TKS)** (approx. 1.5 hours) and return your rental car.
*   **Afternoon:** Use the **JAL Free Domestic Flight Hack**. If you booked your international flight with Japan Airlines, your domestic flight back to **Tokyo Haneda (HND)** is completely free! 
*   **Late Afternoon/Evening:** Arrive at Haneda Airport. Store your heavy luggage in airport lockers, take a quick train into central Tokyo for any last-minute anime shopping or souvenir runs (like Mega Don Quijote), and return to Haneda for your international flight home.

# 💡 Ultimate Budget & Survival Guide for Your Group

1.  **Visit Japan Web:** Fill out your immigration and customs details on the official **Visit Japan Web** website before you land. It generates QR codes that let you breeze through airport immigration digitally.
2.  **Digital IC Cards:** Download a digital **Suica** or **PASMO** card directly to your Apple Wallet or Google Wallet. It makes boarding local trains in Tokyo, Kyoto, and Osaka instant and painless—just tap your phone at the ticket gates.
3.  **Trash Etiquette:** There are virtually no public trash cans in Japan. Carry a small plastic bag in your daypack to hold your trash until you return to your hostel or find a convenience store bin.
4.  **Convenience Store Eats (Konbini):** Do not sleep on **7-Eleven, Lawson, and FamilyMart**. You can get high-quality, hot meals like *Spicy Fried Chicken (L-chiki)*, *Onigiri* (rice balls), and *Pork Katsu Bento Boxes* for under ¥500 ($3.50 USD). It is the ultimate budget-saver for breakfast and quick lunches!
