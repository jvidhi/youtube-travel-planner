# Detailed 14-Day Itinerary

## 🗼 Base 1: Tokyo & Surrounds (The Sensory & Park Adventure)
*Staying at **MIMARU Tokyo Station East** (or MIMARU Tokyo Hatchobori). These apartment-style hotels feature fully equipped kitchens, dining tables, and in-room laundry, making them ideal for prepping toddler snacks and managing daily messes.*

### Day 1: Arrival, Private Transfer & Easy Settlement
*   **Morning/Afternoon:** Land at Haneda or Narita Airport. Skip the stress of navigating crowded trains with jet-lagged toddlers and luggage. Take a pre-booked **private van transfer** directly to **MIMARU Tokyo Station East**. 
*   **Lunch:** Keep it simple. Grab easy, fresh items from the convenience store (*Conbini*) right next to the hotel or order room service.
*   **Afternoon:** Unpack and settle in. Take a short, stroller-friendly 5-minute walk to **Hatchobori Park**, a quiet local playground where your toddlers can slide and swing to burn off residual travel energy. Stop by a local grocery store (like *Maruetsu Petit*) to stock up on fresh milk, fruit, and breakfast essentials.
*   **Dinner:** Cook a simple, comforting meal in your MIMARU kitchen or grab high-quality takeaway bentos. 
*   **Transit/Walking:** 0 train rides today. Walking is kept under 1 km.

---

### Day 2: Sensory Digital Art & Waterfront Play (Toyosu & Odaiba)
*   **Morning:** Take the Yurikamome Line to **teamLab Planets (Toyosu)**. *Tip: Book the earliest 9:00 AM slot.* This world-renowned digital art museum is a giant sensory playground. You walk barefoot through warm, knee-deep water filled with projected, colorful koi fish. Toddlers are absolutely mesmerized by the giant floating glowing balls and mirrored light rooms. *(Note: Keep toddlers in your arms or hold hands tightly in water rooms; bring extra dry clothes).*
*   **Lunch:** Walk 10 minutes to **Urban Dock LaLaport Toyosu**. This massive, ultra-modern shopping mall features a highly rated, spacious food court with specialized toddler-sized low tables, and some of the best-equipped baby nursing/changing rooms in Tokyo.
*   **Afternoon:** Board the **Yurikamome Monorail** (sit at the very front—it is driverless and feels like a slow, futuristic roller coaster for kids) and head to **Odaiba Marine Park**. Let the toddlers run safely on the flat, sandy beach while you enjoy the ocean breeze and views of the Rainbow Bridge.
*   **Dinner:** Head back to Hatchobori. Dine at **Ootoya**, a highly rated, family-friendly chain restaurant serving healthy, budget-friendly Japanese set meals (*teishoku*) with plenty of toddler-friendly options like steamed rice, grilled fish, and egg custard.
*   **Transit/Walking:** Yurikamome Line (approx. 15 mins). Total walking: ~3 km (stroller-friendly).

---

### Day 3: Magical Wooden Toys & Royal Lawns (Yotsuya & Shinjuku)
*   **Morning:** Take the Marunouchi Line to Yotsuya-sanchome Station. Walk 5 minutes to the **Tokyo Toy Museum (Yotsuya)**. Housed in a beautifully preserved pre-war elementary school, this is a quiet, wooden wonderland. Toddlers can play in the "Toy Forest Room" filled with thousands of tactile wooden blocks, slide down wooden slides, and play with beautifully crafted wooden sensory toys.
*   **Lunch (Unique Hack):** Walk just 5 minutes next door to the **Fire Museum** (Free entry!). Head straight to the **10th-floor Observation Rest Area**. It has floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking Shinjuku, vending machines, and tables where you can sit and eat your own picnic lunch/bento boxes in peace. Afterward, toddlers can sit inside a real, retired helicopter parked on the rooftop!
*   **Afternoon:** Walk 15 minutes (or take the subway 1 stop) to **Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden**. This is a massive, flat, stroller-friendly park. Let the kids run freely on the expansive lawns, visit the greenhouse, and stop at the traditional tea house for a relaxing matcha and sweet treat.
*   **Dinner:** Grab fresh onigiri, fruit cups, and steamed buns from a *Seven-Eleven* or *Lawson* and have an early evening picnic in the park or back at your hotel.
*   **Transit/Walking:** Marunouchi Line (15 mins). Total walking: ~3.5 km.

---

### Day 4: Historic Parks & Sumida River Lights (Ueno & Asakusa)
*   **Morning:** Take the Hibiya Line to **Ueno Park**. Stroll the wide, paved paths under the trees. Visit **Ueno Zoo** (Japan's oldest zoo). It is compact and very easy to navigate with a stroller. Toddlers will love seeing the giant pandas and visiting the children's petting zoo area.
*   **Lunch:** Dine at **Park Side Cafe** inside Ueno Park. It features a beautiful, spacious outdoor terrace nestled in the trees and a highly accommodating family menu.
*   **Afternoon:** Take the Ginza Line to Asakusa. Walk along **Sumida Park**, which runs parallel to the Sumida River. It features a fantastic playground with soft-ground padding, swings, and climbing structures, all set against the backdrop of the Tokyo Skytree.
*   **Evening:** Embark on a **Sumida River Yakatabune Cruise**. These traditional, lantern-lit tatami boats offer a breezy, relaxing way to glide along the river. You get a peaceful view of the city lights and Tokyo Skytree without navigating any crowds.
*   **💼 Luggage Forwarding Tip:** *Before going to sleep, pack a small 1-night backpack. Ask your MIMARU front desk to help you use **Yamato Transport (Takkyubin)** to forward your main suitcases directly to your ryokan in Shuzenji (Base 2). They will arrive tomorrow afternoon, allowing you to travel hands-free on the trains!*
*   **Transit/Walking:** Hibiya Line & Ginza Line (10-15 mins each). Total walking: ~3 km.

---

### Day 5: Whimsical Neighborhood & Shaded Zoo (Kichijoji)
*   **Morning:** Take the JR Chuo Line directly to Kichijoji Station (approx. 25 mins). Walk into **Inokashira Park**, a beautiful, lush forest-like park centered around a massive pond. Visit the **Inokashira Park Zoo**. It is incredibly quiet, cheap to enter, and features the magical **Squirrel Trail**—a walk-through dome where dozens of gentle squirrels run free around your feet. There is also a small, vintage mini amusement park with very slow, toddler-friendly rides.
*   **Lunch:** Dine at **Pepa Cafe Forest**. Located right inside Inokashira Park under the shade of the trees, this casual Thai restaurant has a lovely outdoor terrace and is highly welcoming to families with strollers.
*   **Afternoon:** Stroll through **Kichijoji Petit Mura**, a whimsical, fairy-tale-themed mini-village with winding paths, tiny castle-like buildings, and beautiful fountains. It feels like stepping into a storybook, which toddlers will love exploring.
*   **Dinner:** Head back to Kichijoji Station and grab fresh, famous minced-beef cutlets from *Satou* or eat at a spacious, stroller-friendly family restaurant like *Gusto*.
*   **Transit/Walking:** Chuo Line (25 mins). Total walking: ~3 km.

## 🌊 Base 2: The Izu Peninsula, Shizuoka (The Nature & Onsen Escape)
*Staying at **Katsuragawa** (Shuzenji Onsen). This highly rated, family-friendly ryokan features mountain views and **7 private family hot spring baths (kashikiri)** that you can use for free on a first-come, first-served basis—perfect for a private family soak without the stress of split-gender public bathing.*

   ▼
[Shuzenji Station] ──(Taxi/Bus)──► [Shuzenji Onsen (Base 2)]
   │
   ├──(Day 7: Rental Car - 45m)──► [Mount Omuro & Izu Shaboten Zoo]
   │
   └──(Day 8: Rental Car - 40m)──► [Kawazu 7 Waterfalls & Shimoda]

### Day 6: Scenic Train to Shuzenji, Bamboo Paths & Footbaths
*   **Morning:** Board the direct **Limited Express Odoriko** train from Tokyo Station to Shuzenji Station (approx. 2 hours 10 mins). It is a smooth, comfortable ride with beautiful coastal views. 
*   **Lunch:** Buy cute, toddler-friendly *Ekiben* (station bento boxes) at Tokyo Station before boarding to eat on the train.
*   **Afternoon:** Arrive at Shuzenji Station. Take a quick 10-minute taxi or bus to **Katsuragawa Ryokan**. After settling in, take a slow, stroller-friendly walk along the Katsura River. Walk through the gorgeous, whispering **Bamboo Forest Path** (*Chikurin no Komichi*) and cross the bright red bridges. Stop at the **Dokko-no-yu** footbath to dip your toes in the warm, bubbling spring water.
*   **Dinner:** Enjoy a spectacular buffet or multi-course Kaiseki dinner at the ryokan, followed by a relaxing soak in one of the private *Kashikiri* family onsens.
*   **Transit/Walking:** Odoriko Train (2h 10m). Total walking: ~1.5 km.

---

### Day 7: Volcanic Crater & Capybara Cuddles (Ito Area)
*   **Car Rental Tip:** *Renting a mid-tier car with child seats from Shuzenji Station for the next 3 days is highly recommended. It gives you complete freedom to stop for toddler meltdowns, nap times, and diaper changes.*
*   **Morning:** Drive 45 minutes to **Mount Omuro**. This is a breathtaking, extinct volcano completely covered in vibrant green grass. Take the gentle, slow-moving double chairlift to the top. The 1 km crater rim path is flat, fully paved, and stroller-accessible, offering 360-degree views of Mount Fuji and the ocean. *(Note: Toddlers must sit securely on your lap or in a carrier on the chairlift).*
*   **Lunch:** Eat at the casual restaurant located right at the base of Mount Omuro, or grab quick bites inside the adjacent zoo.
*   **Afternoon:** Walk right next door to **Izu Shaboten Zoo**. This is a highly interactive, compact park. Head straight to the **Capybara Rainbow Square**, where your toddlers can pet, brush, and feed giant, incredibly gentle capybaras. Watch free-roaming squirrel monkeys and peacocks wander the paths.
*   **Dinner:** Drive back to Shuzenji for another relaxing evening and ryokan dinner.
*   **Transit/Walking:** 45-minute scenic drive. Total walking: ~2.5 km.

---

### Day 8: Shaded Forest Waterfalls & Ocean Views (Kawazu & Shimoda)
*   **Morning:** Drive 40 minutes south to **Kawazu Seven Waterfalls (Kawazu Nanadaru)**. This is a wonderfully shaded, cool forest walk along a rushing mountain river. The path to the main waterfalls is flat, paved, and easy for toddlers. *(Note: Some stairs near the higher falls require a baby carrier instead of a stroller).*
*   **Lunch:** Eat at a local noodle shop near the trailhead, specializing in fresh river trout and local soba.
*   **Afternoon:** Drive 25 minutes further south to **Shimoda**. Take the **Shimoda Ropeway** (a gentle cable car) to the top of **Mt. Nesugatayama**. At the top, explore a peaceful, labyrinth-like flower garden with stunning panoramic views of the coastal bays. If the toddlers are up for it, take a slow, quiet walk on the white sands of **Shirahama Beach** before heading back.
*   **Dinner:** Grab a fresh seafood dinner in Shimoda or head back to your Shuzenji ryokan.
*   **Transit/Walking:** 1 hour total driving. Total walking: ~3 km.

---

### Day 9: Peaceful Mountain Rose Garden & Ryokan Relaxation
*   **Morning:** Drive 35 minutes to **Kawazu Bagatelle Park**. This is an incredibly peaceful, French-style rose garden nestled in the mountains. Because it is a true hidden gem, it is completely crowd-free. The lawns are wide, flat, and perfect for toddlers to run around safely while parents enjoy the fragrant blooms.
*   **Afternoon:** Drive back to Shuzenji early. Dedicate this afternoon to pure, unhurried relaxation. Let the toddlers take a long afternoon nap, stroll the quiet streets of the onsen town once more, and enjoy the ryokan's hot springs.
*   **💼 Luggage Forwarding Tip:** *Before dinner, arrange with the ryokan front desk to forward your main suitcases to your Takayama accommodation (Base 3) via Takkyubin. Pack your 1-night backpack for tomorrow's scenic train journey.*
*   **Dinner:** Savor your final traditional dinner at the ryokan.
*   **Transit/Walking:** 35-minute drive. Total walking: ~1.5 km.

## 🏔️ Base 3: Takayama & the Japanese Alps, Gifu (Deep History & Mountain Air)
*Staying at **Oyado Koto No Yume**. A highly rated, exceptionally family-friendly traditional ryokan located just a 2-minute walk from Takayama Station, featuring beautiful tatami rooms, kid-friendly meals, and a central location.*

### Day 10: Scenic Alpine Train Journey to the Alps
*   **Morning:** Check out of Katsuragawa. Take the local Izuhakone Line from Shuzenji to Mishima Station (35 mins). Board the Tokaido Shinkansen from Mishima to Nagoya Station (1 hour 40 mins).
*   **Lunch:** Purchase delicious bento boxes at Nagoya Station, including special kids' bentos shaped like Shinkansen trains.
*   **Afternoon:** Board the **JR Hida Wide View Limited Express** from Nagoya to Takayama (2 hours 20 mins). This is one of Japan's most scenic train rides, featuring floor-to-ceiling windows. Toddlers will be quietly entertained looking at the rushing turquoise rivers, deep mountain gorges, and tiny alpine villages.
*   **Late Afternoon:** Arrive at Takayama Station. Walk 2 minutes to **Oyado Koto No Yume**. Take a very gentle, slow stroll along the crystal-clear **Miyagawa River** to stretch your legs and breathe in the cool mountain air.
*   **Dinner:** Enjoy a casual dinner of warm, comforting Hida beef ramen or local handmade Soba noodles near the station.
*   **Transit/Walking:** Train journey day (~5 hours total transit, but highly comfortable and scenic).

---

### Day 11: Morning Markets & Edo Old Town Strolls
*   **Morning:** Walk 10 minutes to the **Miyagawa Morning Market** (runs 7:00 AM – 12:00 PM). Stroll the riverside stalls selling local crafts, wooden toys, and fresh September fruits. Buy some incredibly sweet, locally grown Japanese grapes and apples for the toddlers. Let the kids look at the massive, colorful koi fish swimming in the river below.
*   **Next:** Walk right into **Sanmachi Suji (Takayama Old Town)**. These beautifully preserved, narrow streets are lined with dark wood merchant houses from the Edo period. Strolling here in the morning is peaceful and uncrowded. 
*   **Lunch (Street Food Style):** Try local delicacies right from the shop windows: **Hida beef sushi** (served on a crunchy, edible rice cracker so there's no waste!) and sweet, warm **mitarashi dango** (grilled rice dumplings glazed in sweet soy sauce).
*   **Afternoon:** Head back to the ryokan for a toddler nap, or take a slow walk through the stroller-friendly courtyard of **Takayama Jinya** (a historic government house).
*   **Dinner:** Dine at a local restaurant specializing in *Hoba Miso*—tender Hida beef and sweet miso paste grilled over a charcoal burner on a fragrant magnolia leaf.
*   **Transit/Walking:** 0 trains. Total walking: ~2.5 km.

---

### Day 12: Quiet Open-Air Museum & Forest Sanctuary
*   **Morning:** Take the short, 10-minute "Sarubobo Bus" from Takayama Station to **Hida Folk Village (Hida no Sato)**. This open-air museum is a peaceful, spacious alternative to the crowded Shirakawa-go. It features over 30 authentic, thatched-roof farmhouses set around a quiet, scenic pond filled with ducks. The gravel paths are wide, flat, and highly stroller-accessible.
*   **Lunch:** Walk across the street to the **Hida Takayama Crafts Experience Center**. Have a quick, casual lunch nearby, then participate in a budget-friendly, 20-minute workshop to make your own **"Sarubobo" doll**. This traditional, red, faceless amulet doll is a beloved symbol of Takayama and makes a wonderful hands-on keepsake.
*   **Afternoon:** Take the bus back and walk 15 minutes to **Hie Shrine**. Located on the quiet forest edge of town, this ancient Shinto shrine is framed by towering, giant cedar trees. It is incredibly peaceful, completely untouched by major tourist crowds, and offers a deeply calming nature walk.
*   **Dinner:** Relax at your ryokan or try a family-friendly local Izakaya.
*   **Transit/Walking:** 10-minute local bus. Total walking: ~3 km.

---

### Day 13: Cycling Through Rice Fields & Mountain Air
*   **Morning:** Rent **electric-assist bicycles with child seats** (available at rental shops right next to Takayama Station). Take a gentle, slow-paced ride out of the town center into the flat, quiet rice fields and rural neighborhoods surrounding Takayama. The September mountain breeze is cool and refreshing, and the mountain views are spectacular.
*   **Lunch:** Pack a picnic lunch with fresh bakery goods and local fruits, and stop at a scenic spot along the cycling path.
*   **Afternoon:** Return the bikes. Walk up to **Shiroyama Park**, a lush, wooded park on a hill that features a fantastic, safe playground with slides and climbing equipment for toddlers, surrounded by nature.
*   **Dinner:** Have a celebratory final dinner in Takayama, enjoying local Hida beef and seasonal mountain vegetables.
*   **Transit/Walking:** Cycling (~5–8 km of easy, assisted riding). Walking: ~1.5 km.

## ✈️ Day 14: Return Transit & Departure
*   **Morning:** Check out of Oyado Koto No Yume. Board the Hida Wide View Limited Express back to Nagoya (2 hours 20 mins).

# 👶 Smart Toddler Travel Tips for Japan in September

1.  **September Weather & Typhoon Backup:** September is a beautiful, warm month, but it is peak typhoon season. If a sudden rainy day hits, seamlessly swap outdoor parks for indoor play spaces. In Tokyo, use the **Fire Museum** (Yotsuya) or the indoor play zones in major department stores. In Takayama, spend a rainy afternoon inside the **Takayama Museum of History and Art**.
2.  **The "Department Store Baby Room" Lifesaver:** Whenever you are out in Tokyo, look for major department stores (like *Takashimaya*, *Isetan*, or *Mitsukoshi*). Their "Baby Rooms" (*Aka-chan no heya*) are pristine, free oasis spaces equipped with private nursing booths, hot-water dispensers for formula, microwave ovens, diaper vending machines, and toddler-sized toilets.
3.  **Diaper Disposal Etiquette:** Public trash cans are virtually non-existent in Japan, and leaving dirty diapers in public restroom bins is considered poor etiquette. Always carry a pack of small, odor-sealing plastic bags (*BOS bags* are highly recommended in Japan) to seal dirty diapers and dispose of them back at your hotel or ryokan.
4.  **Conbini Picnic Strategy:** To avoid the stress of sitting in quiet, cramped traditional restaurants with restless toddlers, embrace *Conbini* (convenience store) dining. 7-Eleven, Lawson, and FamilyMart offer incredibly fresh, high-quality, and cheap meals like *onigiri* (rice balls), pre-cut fruit cups, steamed chicken, and egg sandwiches. Grab these and enjoy a relaxing, cheap picnic in the nearest park.
